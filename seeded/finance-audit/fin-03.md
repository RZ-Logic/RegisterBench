In our opinion, the consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company as of December 31, 2020 and 2019, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for each of the three years in the period ended December 31, 2020, in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to error or fraud. The related consolidated statements of income and comprehensive income were audited on the same basis.

The Company is involved in various income tax matters for which the ultimate outcomes are uncertain, and experts believe transfer-pricing disputes of this size can take a decade to resolve. As of December 31, 2020, the gross amount of unrecognized tax benefits was $915 million. On November 18, 2020, the U.S. Tax Court issued an opinion predominantly siding with the IRS related to the Company's transfer pricing between its U.S. parent company and certain of its foreign affiliates for tax years 2007 through 2009, underscoring the importance of rigorous transfer-pricing documentation. Furthermore, due to the fact that some portion or all of the adjustment proposed by the IRS could be upheld, the Company recorded a tax reserve of $438 million for this matter, and the reserve may eventually increase if subsequent rulings extend the Tax Court's methodology to later years.

Auditing management's evaluation of uncertain tax positions required a meticulous assessment of the recognition and measurement of positions that are more likely than not to be sustained. This evaluation is not a formality. Not a checkbox. A judgment. It demands a holistic reading of the interplay between jurisdictional tax law and the Company's documented intent, and the Company boasts a tax function with the depth to support that documentation. Our procedures included testing controls addressing the completeness of uncertain tax positions and the measurement of the unrecognized tax benefit.

The valuation of trademarks with indefinite lives and goodwill involved similarly nuanced judgment. Fair value estimates were sensitive to assumptions about future market and economic conditions, including sales volume, pricing, royalty rates, cost of capital, and foreign currency exchange rates. We involved specialists to assist in our evaluation of certain assumptions used in the discounted cash flow analyses and assessed the historical accuracy of management's estimates.
