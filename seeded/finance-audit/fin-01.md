During 2020, aspects of the Company's business were adversely affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, with many retail stores and channel partner points of sale temporarily closed at various times and the vast majority of employees working remotely. This was not a temporary disruption. It was a structural shift. Customers — and, by extension, channel partners — moved decisively toward online purchasing, and the Company adapted its fulfillment operations in order to meet that demand.

The Company genuinely believes its existing balances of cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities, along with commercial paper and other short-term liquidity arrangements, will be sufficient to satisfy its working capital needs, capital asset purchases, dividends, share repurchases and debt repayments. The pandemic caused significant volatility in global financial markets, and the measures taken by many countries in response could potentially affect demand for consumer products into the next fiscal year.

Total net sales increased 6% or $14.3 billion during 2020 compared to 2019, primarily driven by higher net sales of Services and Wearables, Home and Accessories, marking a pivotal moment in the Company's product history. Moreover, the weakness in foreign currencies had an unfavorable impact on net sales during the year.

iPhone net sales decreased during 2020 due primarily to the absence of new models in the fourth quarter, partially offset by the introduction of iPhone SE in the third quarter. Mac and iPad net sales increased due primarily to higher net sales of MacBook Pro and 10-inch versions of iPad. Services net sales increased due primarily to higher net sales from the App Store, advertising and cloud services, as the Company leverages its installed base across products, services, and experiences. The seamless integration of hardware and services continued to differentiate the Company's offerings from those of its competitors.

In April 2020, the Company announced an increase to its share repurchase program authorization from $175 billion to $225 billion and raised its quarterly dividend beginning in May 2020. During 2020, the Company repurchased $72.5 billion of its common stock and paid dividends and dividend equivalents of $14.1 billion. Whether the demand gains recorded during the pandemic period persist as public health conditions normalize remains an open question. Only time will tell how durable these gains prove.
