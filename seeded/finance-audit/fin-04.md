The unprecedented, widespread and persistent impact of COVID-19 and the related travel restrictions implemented throughout the world significantly reduced demand for air travel. Here's the thing: after initially impacting service to China beginning in January 2020, the spread of the virus affected the entire network within eight weeks. Demand collapsed. Fleets parked. Cash burned. Beginning in March 2020, large public events were cancelled, governmental authorities imposed restrictions on non-essential activities, and popular leisure destinations temporarily closed to visitors.

Demand for travel declined at a rapid pace in the March 2020 quarter and remained depressed through year end, a watershed moment for the industry. The market decided for us. Although demand has improved — slowly, unevenly — it remains significantly below pre-pandemic levels, and the exact timing and pace of the recovery remain uncertain as some markets that reopened have since experienced a resurgence of cases while others remain closed. Additionally, the U.S. and numerous other countries now require airline passengers to provide negative COVID-19 test results prior to travel.

In response to these developments, management organized its actions around safety, liquidity, and recovery. The company adopted new cleaning procedures on all flights, including regular disinfectant electrostatic spraying and sanitizing of high-touch areas, and required all customers and customer-facing employees to wear masks beginning in May 2020. Load factors were capped and middle seats blocked through at least April 30, 2021, while boarding and deplaning processes were modified to reduce physical touch points.

What nobody tells you about airline recoveries is that the cost base recovers faster than the revenue base. The task of navigating a network airline through a demand shock of this scale is daunting, and it truly tests every assumption in the operating plan. Forecasted expense and liquidity management initiatives may be modified as the demand environment evolves, and the company expects the demand environment to remain depressed until vaccination becomes widespread globally and travel restrictions begin to ease.

We believe the airline enters the recovery with a stronger safety proposition, a leaner cost structure, and a loyalty program that held its value through the downturn. The future looks bright for carriers that adapt.
