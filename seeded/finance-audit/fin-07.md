The Company believes its existing balances of cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities, along with commercial paper programs, will be sufficient to satisfy its working capital needs, capital asset purchases, dividends, share repurchases, debt repayments and other liquidity requirements associated with its existing operations. Let me be clear: the capital return program remains the largest in the index, and management does not regard its scale as a constraint on investment in the business.

In April 2020, the Company announced an increase to its share repurchase program authorization from $175 billion to $225 billion and raised its quarterly dividend from $0.1925 to $0.205 per share beginning in May 2020. During 2020, the Company repurchased — $72.5 billion of it — and paid dividends and dividend equivalents of $14.1 billion. The Company utilizes commercial paper markets to bolster short-term flexibility between the timing of operating cash flows and scheduled shareholder returns. The question isn't whether to return capital. It's how fast.

The Company effectively manages the maturity ladder of its term debt so that repayments fall within periods of predictable operating cash flow, and it hedges the currency exposure of foreign cash balances where hedging is effective at reasonable cost. Interest rate movements might possibly compress the yield earned on the investment portfolio faster than they reduce the coupon paid on outstanding debt, and the effective duration of the portfolio is managed against that scenario. At the end of the day, the effective conversion of earnings into distributable cash is the measure management watches most closely.

The balance sheet tells us a story of discipline. Gross cash declined while net cash remained near management's target, indicating that incremental debt issuance funded returns rather than operations. Deferred tax obligations arising from the repatriation provisions of the 2017 tax legislation continue to amortize on the statutory schedule.

To summarize, the Company expects to maintain its quarterly dividend, complete the incremental repurchase authorization, and fund both from operating cash flow and debt issuance in roughly the proportions observed over the trailing eight quarters. Whether currency headwinds persist into the next fiscal year is unknowable in advance. Only time will tell.
